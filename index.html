Sticky Cart and Frequently Bought Together: how shoppers move from click to purchase

Happi Planet product pages. A side-by-side read of the two add-to-cart buttons: how shoppers use them, how often they buy, and the revenue each one brings in.

Period 11–14 Oct 2026 Source GA4 funnel exploration Property Happi Planet (GTM)

What the data says

  • 01

    Sticky Cart is the main way people add to cart. It drew 49,939 button clicks in four days, about six times the 8,061 clicks on Frequently Bought Together (FBT).

  • 02

    Both buttons work as intended. On Tap Cleaner and Kitchen Cleaner, where both are fully tracked, about 97% of clicks on either button become a cart add.

  • 03

    FBT shoppers are more likely to buy. 19.6% of FBT clicks end in a purchase, against 15.1% for Sticky Cart. FBT shoppers have scrolled to the bundle and chosen extra products, so they arrive with stronger intent.

  • 04

    Each FBT add is worth almost three times a Sticky Cart add: about ₹588 of cart value against ₹205. On the Tap Cleaner page, Sticky Cart brings in about 58% of cart value and FBT about 24%.

  • 05

    FBT brings in about a third of the revenue from these two buttons. On Tap Cleaner and Kitchen Cleaner, we estimate ₹2.07 lakh of revenue from FBT and ₹3.49 lakh from Sticky Cart, so FBT drives about 37% of it from only 14% of the clicks.

  • 06

    The biggest drop for both buttons is between cart and checkout. Roughly half of shoppers who add to cart don't start checkout. That cart step is the clearest place to improve.

  • 07

    Tracking is live on Tap Cleaner and Kitchen Cleaner only. Magic Eraser, Washing Machine Cleaner, Copper Cleaner and other pages use older versions of both buttons that don't yet send the add-to-cart event. About three in four clicks on each button happen on those pages, so the funnels and estimates in this report cover Tap Cleaner and Kitchen Cleaner.

FBT gets about 1 in 7 clicks but brings in more than a third of the revenue from these two buttons. Its shoppers buy more often and fill bigger carts, so each FBT click is worth almost four times a Sticky Cart click.

This is an estimate It assumes a shopper who buys pays the cart value they added through the button. Real orders can include other products, discounts or removed items. Purchase counts are shoppers, while cart value is averaged per add. A shopper who used both buttons is counted for both.

Conclusion

Recommendation

Keep FBT on the product pages as it is. It gets only about 14% of add-to-cart clicks, yet it brings in an estimated 37% of the revenue from the two buttons. Each FBT click is worth about ₹115, against ₹31 for a Sticky Cart click.

FBT's lower click share is likely down to a few things rather than the block itself:

  • It's still new. Shoppers are getting used to buying a bundle from the product page.
  • Many shoppers come for one product. Some visitors simply don't want to buy several products in one go.
  • Ad targeting. Campaigns that send traffic to a single product bring single-product intent, which suits Sticky Cart over a bundle.

Even with fewer clicks, FBT competes well on revenue, because the shoppers who use it buy more often and spend nearly three times as much per add.

How this was measured. GA4 funnel exploration, 11–14 Oct 2026 (14 Oct is a partial day), counted by users. Each step must follow the previous one within a set time: click to add to cart within 2 minutes, add to checkout within 30 minutes, checkout to purchase within 1 hour. Sticky Cart adds are identified by the cart_source parameter (sticky_cart). FBT adds use the addtocart_fbt event. FBT cart value is the GA4 Event value of addtocart_fbt events. Cart value on a page is the Event value of all add_to_cart events on that page, which already includes FBT and Sticky Cart adds. Estimated revenue is purchases multiplied by the average cart value per add for that button and page; Sticky Cart cart value is the average pack price on Sticky Cart clicks.

A shopper who used both buttons appears in both funnels, so the two can't be added together. Purchases show that a shopper who used the button went on to buy; they don't yet confirm which items were bought.
